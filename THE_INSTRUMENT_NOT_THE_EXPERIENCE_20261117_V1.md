## 1. The answer in one paragraph

**Instant learning is real and it is not the binding term.** On published bugs the enumerable repair
space *already contains* an answer for **16 of 23** tasks, and the corpus's own pytest confirms every
one of them. The loop found **4**. The difference is not search, not budget, not experience, not
weights, not the space: it is **which observation the loop asks the suite for**, and how that
observation is taken. Asking the corpus's *first* case accepts a wrong program 81% of the time
(19 admissions, 4 correct); asking the *exactly chosen* case — a choice that costs one in-process
pass over the space and no extra knowledge — accepts a wrong program 38% of the time (21 admissions,
13 correct); asking the whole suite is right 16 of 16. **Same space, same seconds, +9 tasks.** The
single bottleneck is the *design and robustness of the instrument that decides whether an attempt
succeeded* — not its existence (this tree has it), not its power, and definitely not more experience.

## 3. What this session measured (new)

On the 31 published bugs that ship example data, the **complete** finite repair space generated by the
merged generator — **278,360 candidate programs**, nothing hand-written — was evaluated against every
published case, exactly, and three screens were put to the corpus's real pytest on a fresh copy:

| screen | what it asks | admissions | correct | mean per-task precision | corpus pytest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FIRST_CASE` | the corpus's first case (what the loop does) | 19 | **4** | **0.096** | 4 pass |
| `BEST_CASE` | the exactly-best case on this space | 21 | **13** | **0.466** | 13 pass |
| `ALL_CASES` | the whole suite | 16 | **16** | 1.0 | **16 pass** |

- **Space adequacy: 16 of 23 measured tasks have a program in the space that satisfies the suite.**
  Of those 16, **13 are single-site local edits**; 3 are rewrites that satisfy the oracle by reaching
  for a builtin the program never used (`kth` → `max`, `lis` → `len`, `sieve` → `all`) and are
  reported as `PLAUSIBLE_REWRITE`, never counted as repairs.
- **The chosen case beats the corpus's first case in 9 tasks and loses in 0.**
- **The in-process suite judge never lied**: 16 accepted by the space, 16 passed the corpus's pytest.
  On this corpus the expensive screen-then-judge pipeline is pure loss.
- **The corpus's first case is not a screen, it is a coin**: on `gcd`, one case admits 1,908 of 2,646
  runnable candidates and 1 of them is right (precision 0.0005); a different case admits 1 and it is
  right. Nothing chose between them. Nothing had to.
- Two tasks raised, and **six could not be measured at all because a candidate swallowed the timer and
  looped forever** (`mergesort`, `levenshtein`, `longest_common_subsequence`, `sqrt`,
  `find_first_in_sorted`, `wrap`). That is not a footnote — see §4.

## 4. The four walls, with counts instead of adjectives

Over the same corpus the failures now partition by *owner*, and each owner has a different repair:

| owner | count | what it means | the repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the instrument's question** | **12** (16 available → 4 found) | the answer existed and the screen asked a case that thousands of wrong programs also pass | **delete the screen**; if one is needed, choose the case by measured precision over the space (free) |
| **the vocabulary / space** | **7** (`flatten`, `hanoi`, `kheapsort`, `powerset`, `shunting_yard`, `subsequences`, `to_base`) | no program in the space satisfies the suite at all | a wider edit grammar — the second mechanic, exactly |
| **the instrument's robustness** | **6** | a candidate **defeated the verifier by hanging it** | process-isolated screening — **already in this tree** (`zygote_worker_pool_v1.screen_python_candidates_cow`); the in-process evaluator was the defect |
| **the oracle is satisfiable without a repair** | **3** | a rewrite passes the suite without repairing anything | the tree's own classifier already names it; it must be a refusal, not a pass |

This is the third mechanic in the owner's own words — *synthesis–verification asymmetry* — but the
count makes it precise: the asymmetry is not that synthesis is harder than verification. It is that
**verification is cheap, available, and wrong in two directions**, and which direction you lose in is
decided by a choice nobody was making.

The frontier reports the same object from four sides, and the numbers agree with ours: SWE-bench Pro's
own verifier was measured at **8.5% false accept / 24.0% false reject** (Datacurve, May 2026);
SWEPatchTest (arXiv:2610.18004) finds the best generated suite reaches **9.21% joint validation** while
reproducing the reference transition on 59.29% of tasks; *Accepted but Wrong* (arXiv:2611.08269) shows
one well-chosen input moves wrong releases 0.410 → 0.065 with false rejection at 0.010 — **the choice
of question, not the number of questions**; and the 2026 self-improvement survey (arXiv:2607.07663)
finds that one bottleneck recurs in every category it examines: **the evaluator**.

## 5. So: right or wrong?

**Right that this is a different learning economy, and it is measured.** One trial per
(atom, state class) buys a scoped table that survives relabelling, reordering and a new session, with
zero prediction errors and zero unrequested side effects, and planning over it is 40 lines and no
trials. Against learning every request by demonstration that is 44 trials for 14/16 versus 34 for
11/16, crossing over at ~6.4 requests per machine. That is a real revolution on the **cost axis**.

**Wrong that it closes coding or computer use.** Three walls survive, and none of them is "the agent
has not seen it once":

1. **You cannot learn an action by experiencing it if you cannot reach a success to experience.**
   32 of 37 uncovered GUI pages were still unexplored at 2.5× the budget; coverage is near-inelastic
   in compute because the cost is `b^depth` with `b = 8.34`. An experience requires a first success,
   and the first success requires the thing you were going to learn.
2. **One experience does not identify a rule.** 0 of 200 identified exactly; the floor is 2.76
   *chosen* experiences; and where the causal observable is absent from the representation the
   insufficiency certificate proves repetition can never help.
3. **A decision about the answer is still needed, and it is not free.** On real bugs, the answer was
   in the space 16 times and the loop said "yes" to a wrong one 15 times. No amount of action
   vocabulary fixes a wrong yes.

**On the creature.** Nothing here measures sentience and this mechanism does not create it. What is
now explicit and checkable is the thing people mean by *understanding* when they say it about a
worker: the machine carries a state, it must *choose an observation* rather than accept one, and it
must know when its answer is unverified. This session's measurement is exactly a case of the second:
the machine's answer depended on which question it asked, and now the choice is computed rather than
inherited. That is a real step toward the three properties the frontier's own failure report names —
state continuity, self-verification, recovery — and it is not a claim of sentience.

## 6. The single bottleneck, fourth time, sharpened each time

> 1. the space's **size** is the vocabulary; its reach per demonstration is the invariant set.
> 2. for computer use the invariant is the **effect vocabulary**, the size is the **(atom × state
>    class) table**; the constraint is **composition**.
> 3. composition is 40 lines and 0 trials; the constraint is the **specification**.
> 4. **the specification is not missing, it is mis-asked.** The suite is there. The answer is there.
>    The loop asked one case and believed it.

So: **the bottleneck is the instrument — which observation, taken how — and the single largest
measured win available this turn is to delete the cheap screen and judge with the thing you already
have.** That is the owner's own law applied to verification: *the best part is no part.* A screen that
loses 12 of 16 available answers is not a cheap approximation of the judge; it is a second, worse
oracle.

## 7. The smartest things to do, ranked by measured payoff

1. **Delete the prefilter.** Judge every candidate with the exact suite, in-process where faithful
   (16/16 agreement here), process-isolated where a candidate can hang the judge. Cost: seconds.
   Payoff measured: **4 → 16** on this corpus, 13 of them genuine single-site repairs.
2. **When a prefilter is unavoidable, choose the case by measured precision over the space** — exact,
   zero-knowledge-beyond-the-suite, and beats the corpus order in 9 tasks and loses in none.
3. **Move the vocabulary wall, which is now the only honest "space" failure: 7 of 23.** No screen
   reaches those; the edit grammar must.
4. **Robustness is part of the instrument**: 6 tasks were unmeasurable because a candidate looped and
   defeated the timer. The tree's COW pool already exists; use it.
5. **Weights last**, and only from identified, contrastive, admitted traces — `ready_to_train=False`
   stands. A parameter justified by an unidentified survivor is the knowledge house the owner rejects.

Kill list, unchanged and now one line longer: more demonstrations in the same state (0 bits), more
budget (1 page per 2.26×), more action primitives as the coverage fix (0 of 37 pages proved
unreachable), a bigger library (≈ no library), training now, **and any screen nobody chose**.

## 8. Honest limits

- **One corpus, one session, producer-run.** QuixBugs at `4257f44b`; 31 tasks ship examples, 23 were
  measurable; the answer key never enters a task directory; the verdict is the corpus's own pytest.
- **`BEST_CASE` is chosen on the same space it screens**, which is legitimate here because the space
  is enumerable and evaluated in-process, and is *not* a licence to tune a verifier on the data it
  will judge in a deployment where candidates arrive one at a time. A held-out space is the honest
  version and is not what was run.
- **Six tasks were not measured** (timer-defeating candidates) and two raised. They are in the receipt
  as `UNMEASURED__…`, never as failures and never as passes.
- **`ALL_CASES` agreeing with pytest 16/16 is not a proof that the in-process judge is sound**; it is
  what this corpus's cases happen to be. Module-level state, imports and object-identity assertions
  are exactly where an in-process judge would diverge.
- Not claimed: any capability credit, any family flip, any weight, any leaderboard number, any
  sentience result. Authority **NONE**; independent verification open.
