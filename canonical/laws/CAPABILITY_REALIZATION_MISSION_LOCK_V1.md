## Terminal objective

Project Brain exists to make the **actual target capabilities** demonstrated by Astra real in our own system, using the right architecture, tools, models, programs, evidence, and execution routes available to us.

The objective is capability realization, not imitation of Astra's implementation and not possession of the Astra label.

## What counts as progress

Progress exists only when at least one of these is true:
1. a target capability is materially stronger in real use;
2. a previously unavailable target capability becomes genuinely executable;
3. reliability, breadth, speed, autonomy, or generalization of a target capability improves in a way that matters to real tasks;
4. a causal blocker to such capability realization is falsified or removed.

## What does NOT count as the goal

The following are instruments only:
- predicates;
- benchmarks;
- qualification suites;
- task counts;
- worker counts;
- identities;
- infrastructure;
- architecture documents;
- orchestration systems;
- proofs and verifier receipts;
- model or platform labels.

They may measure, enable, or falsify capability. They MUST NOT substitute for capability.

## Predicate subordination law

A predicate may be created or executed only when it answers a live capability question that directly affects the target objective.

Passing a predicate never creates the capability by definition.
Failing a predicate never defines the capability boundary by definition.
Predicates are evidence about reality; reality is not evidence about predicates.

## Benchmark subordination law

External or internal benchmarks are diagnostic instruments. They are useful for measuring strength, breadth, generalization, reliability, or regressions.

Benchmark parity is not the terminal objective.
Benchmark completion is not sufficient evidence that the target capabilities are fully real.
Benchmark work that does not change a live capability decision is drift.

## Astra relationship

Astra is a capability reference, not an implementation blueprint and not a naming target.

We seek the useful capabilities Astra demonstrates, realized in our own system by the right route.

Do not:
- clone Astra merely for resemblance;
- optimize for its label;
- treat internal Astra-named predicates as the mission;
- require architectural similarity to Astra unless causally necessary.

## Mandatory pre-action question

Before every consequential action ask:

> Does this action directly make a target Astra capability more real, stronger, broader, more reliable, more autonomous, or remove the current blocker to doing so?

If NO, abort unless the action is strictly necessary evidence preservation or safety/correctness verification for a selected capability action.

## Anti-proxy regression

The following are permanent failures:
- declaring success because an internal qualification suite is complete;
- scaling workers whose capability strength has not been shown to advance the target;
- building infrastructure because it is measurable rather than because it removes the current capability blocker;
- optimizing benchmark scores instead of the underlying capability;
- treating a predicate ledger as the project roadmap.

## Priority

When proxy optimization conflicts with real capability realization, capability realization wins.

When the fastest route and the correct route conflict, choose the correct route.
Among correct routes, choose the one with minimum wall-clock time to real verified capability.
