## 1. What happened

I committed a scaling law last round: `questions ~ states**0.41`, with accuracy after acquisition
falling from 0.678 at depth 2 to 0.596 at depth 4, and the conclusion that *acquisition is a refinement
that needs a dense base, not a bootstrap*.

Then, building the follow-up instrument, one number disagreed with an older receipt for the same machine
and seed — 0.94 against 0.72 on GIT at walk 120. The difference was one line:

```python
conditions = pop.condition_pool([b for _i, b, _a in full])    # mine: every enumerated state
conditions = pop.condition_pool([b for _i, b, _a in walked])  # every other receipt: what the walk saw
```

**I built the condition vocabulary out of states the learner had never visited.** `full` is the entire
depth enumeration, including the held-out states. The vocabulary is a feature set the learner is
supposed to obtain from what it observed; feeding it the enumeration means a parameter carried world
facts the system had not earned — the §2 forbidden shape, arrived at through the feature set instead of
through the weights. Worse, the held-out transitions are then scored *in a vocabulary borrowed from
themselves*.

I did not catch this by re-reading my code. I caught it because a number disagreed with an earlier
number and I stopped to find out why.

## 2. The measurement, corrected

Same machines, same seed, same depths, only the vocabulary source changed:

| depth | states | questions | per 100 states | accuracy — **leak** | accuracy — **correct** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 76 | 38 | 50.0 | 0.678 | **0.920** |
| 3 | 230 | 88 | 38.3 | 0.618 | **0.906** |
| 4 | 771 | 142 | 18.4 | **0.596** | **0.932** |

The leak did not shift the numbers by a constant. **It reversed the sign of the conclusion.** Under the
leak, precision declined with depth; with the vocabulary the learner could actually have, precision is
flat — 0.920, 0.906, 0.932, a spread of 2.7 points across a **ten-fold** increase in state-space size.

`questions ~ states ** 0.58` (exponents 0.758 then 0.396).

## 3. What survives, what dies

**Survives:** the supply grows monotonically (38 → 88 → 142) and sublinearly; density falls with depth
(50.0 → 38.3 → 18.4); the supply is always finite inside a 400-probe budget; the exponent is between
0.4 and 0.76 depending on the interval, so "sublinear" is solid and "0.41" was not.

**Dies:** *"acquisition is a refinement that needs a dense base to refine"* — that mechanism was the
leak talking. The thing I described as precision collapsing with scale was a learner being graded in a
language built from pages it had not been allowed to read.

**Also dies, and this one stings more:** the whole premise of the follow-up instrument — *can density
predict how accurate acquisition will be?* — presupposed that accuracy varies with depth. It does not.
There is nothing to predict. A free-feature predictor "succeeded" under the leak (mean absolute error
0.0055, trained on depths 2–3, tested on 4) and that success was the leak's signature: `A_accuracy`
predicted `C_accuracy` almost perfectly because both were being measured in borrowed terms. I had this
written up as the first predictive instrument in the project. It was the first artifact of a bug.

That is the useful lesson and it is not subtle: **the more beautifully a predictor fits, the more it
deserves suspicion.** A near-perfect fit between "accuracy before probes" and "accuracy after probes"
should have read as *something is shared between them that should not be* — because with a legitimate
vocabulary, acquisition either adds information or it does not, and it cannot nearly equal the
walk's own score for free.

## 4. Verified / unverified / failed

**Verified by execution:** the two-pool table above; the reversal of sign; flat accuracy under the
correct pool; monotone sublinear growth of the supply under both pools.

**Failed, mine, twice in two rounds:** the accuracy-gated stopping rule (previous round) and the
`0.41` scaling claim with its "refinement not bootstrap" mechanism (this round). Both are recorded as
losses rather than tuned away.

**Unverified:** the exponent. Two intervals give 0.758 and 0.396; a third measurement at depth 5 did not
finish in a defensible time. "Sublinear, somewhere between 0.4 and 0.8" is what I am entitled to say.

## 5. What is actually left as a result

Three rounds of asking questions have produced one durable, negative, honest finding and one law:

1. **The selection policy is not a lever** (three principled rules tie within noise).
2. **The budget is a lever only up to the size of the question supply**, and the supply is finite
   (≈40–140 probes) and scales sublinearly (0.4–0.8) with the state space.
3. **Precision does not decay with scale**, once the vocabulary is honest. Acquisition on top of a walk
   holds 0.90–0.93 across a ten-fold change in depth.

(3) is the only encouraging number the project has produced, and I only got it by distrusting a better
one.

Every tool that builds a condition pool now takes `--conditions {walk,full}` so this class of leak is a
parameter with a visible default rather than a silent line. The next real test is not another acquisition

walk. If precision holds at 0.9 across machines as it now holds across depths, that is the first claim in
this body of work that is worth something outside these ten machines.
