# The verifier pins 71% of the change. You were told it was 100%.

Round 15. Branch `arena/17762d9d-brain`, from `89b8f8b3`.

## The word nobody audited

Since round 9, every receipt in this project ends with the same sentence: *the commit's own tests pass*.
That sentence has been read as "the change is correct", and every claim about instant learning, retention
and permanence has been built on top of it.

This round audits the word, and the audit is cheap because it needs no new data. **Revert one part of the
human's own change and run the suite.** That is not a synthetic mutant; it is a subset of real work. If
the suite still passes, then the verifier would have accepted the fix *with that part missing* — it would
have certified a bug.

## What came back

16 real fixes, 77 suite executions, 45 parts of real changes:

| | |
|---|---|
| parts the verifier **requires** (reverting them turns the suite red) | **30 of 45** |
| behavioural parts pinned | **30 of 42 = 71.4%** |
| fixes with at least one behavioural part the suite accepts without | **6 of 16** |
| worst single fix | **7 of 8 parts reversible with the suite still green** |
| tasks refused because the controls did not hold | 1 (reported, not counted) |

The worst case is `hukkin/tomli` `67ec7d10`. Every one of its parts rewrites an error:

```python
raise TOMLDecodeError(suffix_location(state, "Can not overwrite a value"))
# part reverted:
raise TOMLDecodeError(...)
```

The suite passes either way, because it asserts the **exception type** and never the **message**. So the
verifier pins *that something raises*, and is silent about *what it says* — while the entire content of
that fix is what it says. An agent could learn that fix, be wrong in seven of its eight parts, and be
certified.

`pallets/itsdangerous` `7f4dcf83` shows the second mechanism: the same line
`default_digest_method: t.Any = staticmethod(_lazy_sha1)` appears twice, once in a class the suite
exercises and once in a class it does not. One copy is pinned. The other is free.

## Why this is the answer to the question you keep asking

You asked whether giving the model the smartest actions and letting it experience them once makes it
permanent. The retention half is real and free — that is settled. **This round settles what "verified"
was standing in for, and the answer is: a property of the assertions, not of the learner.**

No amount of experience, memory, replay, or smarter actions raises 71.4%. That number is set by what the
tests can see. Which means:

> "Learned once, verified, kept forever" is exact about what was **stored** and silent about whether what
> was stored was **observed**.

That is the third face of the same wall the last three rounds measured, and now the three numbers compose:

| question | answer | round |
|---|---|---|
| can the agent build a candidate at all? | **1 of 17** real fixes | 11–14 |
| does the verifier prefer the right candidate? | **0 of 4** (four distinct repairs pass) | 13 |
| is the accepted change actually observed? | **71.4%** of behavioural parts | 15 |

Multiply them and you get the honest confidence an agent may place in its own work. Each term is a
property of the *world* — evidence, identity, observation — and none is a property of the learner.

There is also a clean, uncomfortable observation in the composition: the **only fully pinned fix in the
corpus is `022ae6c6`, and it is exactly the one where four different repairs pass.** Pinning and identity
are independent. A suite can require every part of your change and still not require *your* change.

## The capability this gives, which is not a verdict

Per-part certification. The agent can now say, for its own work, which parts are pinned and which are
hypotheses. That is a behaviour change, not a measurement: an unpinned part must be reported as unverified
rather than shipped as done — the same discipline this round applied to the humans' fixes, applied to ours.

And it points at the highest-leverage zero-model move left in the whole architecture, which is *not*
another learner and *not* more search:

**Turn each unpinned behavioural part into the assertion that would pin it, then verify that the new
assertion fails on the reverted part and passes on the fix.** That is assertion synthesis from the change
itself — mechanical, no model, no network, and it *raises* the pinning share, because the assertion is
generated by exactly the difference the current suite cannot see. It is the one move in this project that
makes the verifier stronger instead of making the agent more confident.

## What would be dishonest to claim

* That the suites are bad. They are ordinary suites; the gap is structural — assertions are cheaper than
  the behaviour they stand for, everywhere, in everyone's code.
* That 71.4% is the agent's accuracy. It is the *certified* share of real changes; the agent's accuracy is
  a different number and it is lower.
* That pinning solves identity. The fully pinned fix is the least identified one in the corpus.
* That this is a defect of instant learning. It is what instant learning is: perfect transmission of a
  record, plus no opinion about whether the record was observed.
