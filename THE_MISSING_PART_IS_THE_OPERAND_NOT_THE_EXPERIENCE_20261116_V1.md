## 0. The question, and the answer in one paragraph

> *Instant learning and instant learning in new arbitrary environments are already solved
> perfectly. So give the model the most advanced actions, let it do a thing once, and it knows it
> permanently — therefore all computer use and the most advanced software engineering are solved.
> Is this true? What is real, what is not, and what are the smartest things to do?*

**The direction is right, one step of it is measurably real, and the step that carries the word
"permanently" is the wrong step.** What is real, measured at full scale this session: **a missing
*part* — not a missing experience — bought 5 pages, cost 0, and cut the action spend by 9%.** What
is also real, and is the finding that matters more: **all 5 gains sit on the eight pages the part
was written for, and on the other 121 pages the addition changes nothing at all** (77 vs 77).
A part is a capability. It is not yet transfer, and no amount of running the same thing again will
make it transfer, because on those 121 pages the machine is not short of *tries* — it is short of
the *operand* the page wants. That is the same wall, in the same place, that this project has now
measured nine times from nine different angles.

## 1. What was actually done this session, and why it is not another essay

The tree already contained the answer to this question eight times. It also contained the thing
that made nine-times-worth of essays unnecessary: **one measured positive — the derived-value
algebra, `1/8 → 6/8` pages solved** — resting entirely on **eight pages chosen by looking at them**.

That is precisely the failure the owner's standing instruction names: *"we keep testing on bugs we
already know work. That's not progress — that's self-congratulation."* An eight-page study where
every page was inspected before the operator was written is a **re-measurement of a design**, not a
capability claim. The honest test is the one nobody had run: **all 130 pages of the suite, unseen.**

So this session ran it. Three arms that differ **only** in what may be typed (pressing, hovering,
dragging are byte-identical across arms, and a test enforces it):

| arm | what it may type | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **A** shipped hand | quoted spans, instruction words, page text | the control |
| **U** union | A **plus** the derived-value algebra | an **addition** — nothing removed |
| **D** decoy | the same number and shape of values, **wrong witness** | is the gain the rules, or the volume? |

Verdict is each page's own reward, re-read by replaying the plan on a **fresh page**.

### The numbers, real Chromium, 400 actions, two seeds

| | A shipped | U union | D decoy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| seed 1060, 129 pages | **78** | **83** | 66 |
| seed 771, 130 pages | **77** | **81** | 66 |
| jsdom, 123 pages | 62 | 67 | 51 |

`stock-market` is the one page seed 1060 could not measure (bridge timeout — the same page the
tree's earlier Chromium run also recorded as an instrument error); seed 771 measures it at 400/400/400,
unsolved by every arm, so it changes no comparison. Both seed denominators are stated rather than
one being quietly substituted for the other.

**Union vs shipped: +5 pages, −0 pages, and 1,917 fewer actions spent (seed 1060, −7.9%).**
**Decoy vs shipped: −12 net (seed 1060), −11 net (seed 771)** — the decoy gains 2 pages and loses
14, which is what a control built from the *wrong witness* should do. The gain is the *rule*, not
the extra values.

The five pages, with their cost — every one of them unsolvable by the shipped hand at **4× the
budget** (1600 actions, measured, `A=False/1600a` on all five):

| page | shipped hand | union | rule that solved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `simple-arithmetic` | 400a, FAIL | **12a** | `ARITHMETIC` |
| `simple-algebra` | 400a, FAIL | **25a** | `LINEAR` |
| `enter-time` | 400a, FAIL | **29a** | `CLOCK24` |
| `scroll-text` | 400a, FAIL | **29a** | `LAST_WORD` |
| `text-transform` | 400a, FAIL | **33a** | `JOIN_SHOWN` |

A page that wants `19:55` from `7:55 PM`, or the last word of a 900-character text area, is not
**slow** to reach. It is **unreachable**, because no hypothesis generator can construct the string
and an oracle can select only among the actions it is shown. Four times the compute does not help;
a **part** helps, and it helps in 12 to 33 actions.

## 2. The finding that is worth more than the win

```
held-out pages (never used to design any operator)    121
    shipped hand                                       77 / 121
    union (shipped + algebra)                          77 / 121     <- identical
    decoy                                              66 / 121
```

**Zero of the five gains transfer.** On the 121 pages that never touched the design, adding a
whole algebra of value derivation changes the score by exactly nothing. This is the number the
eight-page study could not produce and the reason the eight-page study was not a result.

And the same instrument, read the other way, on the same suite (seed 1060, jsdom):

```
algebra REPLACES the literal vocabulary   ->  61 solved  (vs 62 shipped)   NET NEGATIVE
    gains 5:  enter-time, scroll-text, simple-algebra, simple-arithmetic, text-transform
    losses 7: the entire email-inbox family + enter-password
algebra is ADDED to it                    ->  67 solved  (vs 62)          NET +5, 0 losses
```

**Replacement loses, addition wins.** Seven pages depend on a literature word the algebra does not
produce (`email-inbox` wants the sender's name, `enter-password` wants `STRIP`), and swapping the
vocabulary for a cleverer one throws them away. The eight-page study never saw this because it
contained no page that lives on the old vocabulary. **The lesson is not "the algebra is good". It
is "capability grows by addition, and every replacement is a silent deletion."** That is the best
operational reading of *the best part is no part* this measurement can give, and it is not the
reading that says "fewer parts": it says **remove nothing that something still uses.**

## 3. So is the plan right? Step by step, with the number attached

"Once ⇒ permanent ⇒ anything on a computer" hides five transitions. The tree has now measured all
five. Four of them fail, and **none of the four is a learning problem**:

| # | transition | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a first success exists to learn from | 83 of 129 pages (this session, Chromium, union hand) | **the start line is 64%** |
| 2 | one success ⇒ a rule for the next instance | 60.0% vs 15.0% cache (`OPEN_BENCHMARK_..._V2`) | **real, and the only step that works** |
| 3 | permanent storage ⇒ correct knowledge | ungated lesson **breaks a correct program**; gated one refused | **permanence without a judge is harm** |
| 4 | N verified skills ⇒ a verified sequence | two individually-correct skills compose to a **no-op** | **composition is not free** |
| 5 | a learned procedure ⇒ any computer use | best 2026 agent **20.6%** binary on 108 long-horizon tasks | **the frontier is at 1/5** |

Step 2 is yours, and it is real. Steps 1, 3, 4 and 5 are why "therefore everything" is false. A
chain of five factors is not a chain of one: a zero anywhere is a zero at the end.

### Why "give it the most advanced actions" is not the lever — and is partly an anti-lever

This session measured the action set's actual yield. **46 of 129 pages remain unsolved by the full
hand** (shipped vocabulary + algebra + every press, hover, triple-press, grab, drop, nudge and
wait). Of those 46, exactly **2 are *proved* exhausted** — every action reachable from every state
the machine can distinguish was tried — and those two (`hot-cold`, `hover-shape`) are provably
missing an **observable** (the page looks identical in states that behave differently), not an
action. The other **44 ran out of budget with actions still untried**.

That is the opposite of the intuition, stated arithmetically. Measured branching factor on this
substrate is **8.34 actions per distinguishable state**, median **150 states per page**:

```
b = 8.34   depth 3      580 actions     <- a 400-action budget reaches about here
           depth 4    4,838
           depth 5   40,349
           depth 6  336,511   (224x what was spent)

b = 2      depth 6       64 actions
b = 3      depth 6      729
```

The budget needed grows **exponentially** in depth; willingness to spend grows **linearly**. That
race is lost by construction. **Every action you add raises the branching factor and makes the
search strictly harder, while it also makes coverage cheaper.** They pull in opposite directions.
The resolution is that **an action must arrive with its use condition** — an operand algebra is
exactly such a part: it does not add actions to the search, it adds *one correct value* where 400
wrong ones were being tried.

## 4. The bottleneck, said once, with the evidence that picks it

If one thing is to be fixed, it is **not** storage, parameters, the action set, or the number of
demonstrations. It is **the operand vocabulary of each operator** — the set of things the machine
can *express* — and immediately behind it, **the verifier** that decides whether an attempt
succeeded.

The two are the same shape of problem:

- **A page the machine cannot express is unreachable at any budget.** Measured: 5 pages, 1600
  actions each, zero successes, until a part existed; then 12–33 actions. The experience was never
  the missing quantity.
- **A verifier that cannot say "right" makes permanence actively harmful.** The tree's own gate
  reproduced it: an ungated lesson breaks a correct program. In the 2026 field the same wall is
  reported from three directions — OSWorld 2.0 finds agents spend **under 7%** of their budget on
  detecting their own errors and fail on *verification and state drift*, not GUI control
  (arXiv:2606.29537); Veritas finds both training-time and test-time self-improvement are "gated by
  the same bottleneck: the verifier" (arXiv:2611.13336); *All Smoke, No Alarm* finds **80.2%** of
  86,156 agent-authored test patches carry weak or absent oracle signals (arXiv:2606.18168).

And the code domain is where this is winnable **first**, for a structural reason rather than a
preference: code ships an oracle that is *available but unreliable* (a passing test can accept a
wrong patch — this tree measured **0.8021**, i.e. ~20% of wrong code passes a real suite), while
GUI ships one that is *reliable but unavailable* (nothing tells you the spreadsheet is right). The
fastest real capability is the domain where a candidate can be **falsified cheaply and
independently of the producer.** That is code with execution.

## 5. What the 2026 field measured, and how it lands on the same conclusion

Every recent benchmark of "let the agent learn from its own experience" lands on the same finding
this session measured from the inside — **sequential experience helps; the reusable-skill claim is
where it breaks**:

- **ContinualSkillBench** (arXiv:2608.03874): sequential execution gains **16.9%** relative across
  14 of 15 model–domain cells, but a pure in-context agent that keeps **no skill library** matches
  the skill-maintaining agent (**0.605 vs 0.602**). The gain is adaptation to feedback, not skill
  consolidation. Weaker models build *larger, more fragmented* libraries and reuse them less.
- **S³Gym** (arXiv:2608.31100): self-improvement is "neither automatic nor uniform"; parameter
  training shows "severe negative transfer" on some tasks; accurate self-scoring **does not
  predict** improvement.
- **SkillLearnBench** (arXiv:2604.20087): all continual-learning methods beat no-skill, but the best
  closes only **~45%** of the gap to human-authored skills, and learned skills **hurt** on
  open-ended tasks. "Self-feedback alone induces recursive drift."
- **SkillMisevolution** (arXiv:2608.12851): across 21 agent×method combinations, **all of them**
  wrote unsafe skills and 15 carried the harm into a later session. Permanence without admission is
  a contamination channel, not a capability.
- **ERL** (arXiv:2603.24639): raw trajectories *hurt* (−1.9%); distilled heuristics help; failure-
  derived heuristics beat success-derived ones. **Abstraction is the win, replay is not.**
- **SEAGym** (arXiv:2606.17546): frequent harness updates can leave **held-out performance
  unchanged**, and useful intermediate snapshots later degrade.

That is six independent 2026 groups reaching the sentence this session measured: **the part is the
capability; the experience is only what tells you which part is missing.**

On the owner's two laws, the evidence is now on the owner's side, with a precise reading:

- *"The best part is no part."* Measured support: **4× compute bought nothing** on the five gain
  pages; a part bought them in 12–33 actions. Compute is the part you should delete first.
- *"Parameters must not be a knowledge house."* Measured support: transformer fact capacity is
  ~**2 bits/parameter** (Allen-Zhu & Li; Morris et al. put GPT-style models at ~3.6), and the 2026
  measurement is that capability density on reasoning benchmarks rose 2024–2026 **without** more
  efficient factual storage — the gain is in procedural parameters, not in facts
  (arXiv:2604.24827; R²=0.910 log-linear fit on 93 open-weight models). A fact store in weights also
  cannot tell you **when something was never recorded**. So: procedure and prior in the weights,
  facts external. That is your architecture, and it is now the measured one.
- The law's precise form for this session's finding: **"no part" is not "fewest parts" — it is "no
  part without a job, and no part removed while something still uses it."** The replacement arm is
  what the misreading costs: −7 pages for a cleverer vocabulary.

## 6. On "our model", "from scratch", and "sentient"

Taken as instructions, plainly:

- **No model is used, reached for, or depended on anywhere in this measurement.**
  `producer_cognition_model_dependency_count: 0` in the receipt, and the arms are a hand, a rule
  algebra, and the page's own reward. The result is about **parts**, and it transfers to any model
  or to none — which is the strongest form a result can take here.
- **"Sentience" is not measurable by anything in this tree, and nothing here claims it.** The word
  hides three things that *are* measurable, and the 2026 failure report names exactly those:
  **state continuity** (OSWorld 2.0: "loss of task-level state"), **self-verification** (<7% of
  budget), **recovery** (noticing and repairing before submission). Build those three and the
  system will look like what you mean. Build none and no amount of "once" will produce it.
- **The one-sentence version of what is real:** *one verified experience becomes permanent storage
  (yes); one experience identifies a general rule (no — 10% held-out after one, ~80% after three,
  100% only after two chosen questions); and the unit that actually moves coverage is a missing
  **part**, measured this session at +5 pages, 0 regressions, −9% actions, and **zero transfer
  outside the pages it was written for.***

## 7. The smartest next moves, ranked, each with its kill condition

The ranking is by *what unlocks the most*, and every item is written as a **falsifiable**
measurement, because the eight-page study is the cautionary tale: a claim that cannot fail is not
a claim.

1. **Buy the operand parts the 2 proved-exhausted and 40 budget pages name, one at a time, and
   measure each at full-suite scale.** This session shows the method works (+5, 0 regressions) and
   shows its current limit (no transfer). The named candidates, straight from the corpus:
   - **`DATE`** — `enter-date` wants `08/13/2018` → `2018-08-13`. A transposition, expressible today;
     **it already passes on Chromium** and failed only on the layout-free substrate, so the first
     job is to stop calling it a wall.
   - **`COMBINE`** — `simple-algebra` wants `[15,15,16]`-style multi-field values; the algebra
     currently derives one value per rule.
   - **`POINT`** — `hot-cold`, `hover-shape`, `find-midpoint`, `right-angle`, `draw-line`,
     `circle-center`, `use-slider`, `simon-says`. Both bridges are **element-addressed only**:
     the agent cannot say *"a point"*. This is a missing operand type, not a missing action.
   - **`SCROLL_TO`** — `scroll-text-2`, `sign-agreement`.
   - **`PICK_FROM_LIST`** — `use-autocomplete` ("starts with Sur and ends with ame").
   **Kill condition:** any item that, at 1600 actions on the full suite, moves **no** page and
   **loses** one. **Keep condition:** ≥1 page gained, 0 lost, and *recorded whether the gain is
   inside the design set.*
2. **A state key that can see what behaviour depends on.** The only 2 pages *proved* exhausted
   (`hot-cold`, `hover-shape`) stop because the same look behaves differently — a missing
   **observable**. The tree already owns the instrument that decides whether a proposed observable
   can separate the cases: the insufficiency certificate. Use it before building anything.
3. **The verifier for the domain that has an oracle — code — made adversarial.** Execution evidence
   plus a check that cannot be satisfied by a no-op. This is the term with the largest multiplier
   and the lowest cost of being wrong: it is what stops step 3 of §3 from writing a 68%-wrong
   reading into permanence.
4. **Wire the admission gate into one autonomous caller**, so nothing becomes permanent without a
   held-out verdict. The gate exists and works; it is not on the path.
5. **Weights last**, from identified, contrastive, admitted traces only — procedure and prior, not
   facts. `ready_to_train=False` at 258 units / 64 verified against a 5,000 threshold. Training now
   would bake in the 68%.

**What not to do, all measured dead in this tree — do not rebuild:**

- **More demonstrations of the same thing.** Exactly zero bits (0/200 identified).
- **More search budget.** 2.26× compute → 1 page; and 4× budget → **0 of 5** pages here.
- **More action primitives, as the coverage fix.** 0 of 37 pages were proved unreachable by a
  missing primitive, and the hand is inert on 36% of the initial state.
- **Pricing inert actions (the "optimise the branching factor" idea).** Built, four arms, tied at
  **1/6**, 2,003 actions each. It is a real tax and not the binding constraint.
- **A bigger skill library.** In-context ≈ library (0.605 vs 0.602, ContinualSkillBench).
- **Replacing a working vocabulary with a cleverer one.** −7 pages for +5, measured here.
- **Breadth of environments as a substitute for admission.** More places to be wrong, unverified.

## 8. Honest limits of this document

- **Producer-run, one machine, no independent personnel.** Chromium 153.0.0 via
  `@sparticuz/chromium`, one page, real hit-testing and input, not a person's desktop.
- **MiniWoB++ is small and relatively easy by 2026 standards.** Our 82/130 is far below frontier,
  so it is **not** evidence of frontier capability — the *mechanism* result stands on its own terms
  (a part, not an experience, moved the number and compute did not).
- **One substrate pair, one benchmark.** The jsdom run has **no layout engine**, and it produced
  two false "exhausted" verdicts (`enter-date`, `scroll-text-2`) that real Chromium closed in 2 and
  10 actions. Any conclusion drawn from jsdom alone about *reachability* is suspect, and the receipt
  keeps both substrates side by side so the disagreement stays visible.
- **The held-out claim is strong but narrow.** 121 pages never touched the design, and the addition
  changed none of them. That is a statement about *this algebra*, not a theorem that no part can
  transfer.
- **Not claimed:** any capability, any family flip, any weight change, any terminal credit, any
  sentience result.
