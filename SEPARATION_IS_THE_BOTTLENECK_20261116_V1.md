## 0. The answer in one paragraph

**You are right about the first capability and wrong about the word "therefore".** Instant storage and instant application of a demonstrated *operation inside its premise* are real and measured here. One experience does not buy a correct rule, a complete action basis, or a correct verdict in a new environment. In a real program space, one demonstration leaves a median of **1,037** surviving programs — **10.018 bits** unresolved; on 479 real code sites it licenses **0** requirements and would act wrongly on **297 / 319** held-out sites (precision **0.069**). The honest single bottleneck is **separation**: the pair `(a hypothesis space that contains the truth, an observation that splits it)`, with an oracle that can actually read the split. On all 40 published bugs measured today, the binding factor is the **space**, and no fixed vocabulary covers it: two independent mechanisms cover a **pytest-rejudged union of 24 / 40**, each under half alone. PR #6887 re-ran the learned arm from its recorded line changes: 16 of 20 claimed repairs were confirmed, 4 were not reconstructible, and the union fell from the projected 26 to the verified 24. On the newest real-code measurement, the binding factor is the **observable vocabulary** — 18 sites are identical in all ten static facts and differ, so the missing observation is the operation's own proof. On the nine tasks with no JSON examples, making the test file itself the observable made them searchable and repaired **0 / 9**; three candidates that the fast screen accepted were refused by the published pytest. On 100 held-out HumanEvalFix bugs, majority vote among test-passing candidates chose the wrong repair in **3 of 5** disagreements, while a chosen labelled question settled **8 / 8** correctly with **0** regressions. The observable was necessary and still not the binding term; agreement is not identification. The "verifier", "goal", "observable", "edit space", and "admission" answers in this repository were correct measurements of one factor each, each mistaken for the whole. The engineering form of keeping separation in an open world is not a new learner: it is a **proof-carrying counterfactual loop** that assembles an operation and its meaning-preserving premise from the failure's own evidence, derives a falsifiable verdict from live execution, and lets an independent witness kill the candidate. That loop's parts already exist in this tree under non-obvious names; what was missing was the equation, the honest open-task population, and the discipline not to count old wins.

**Is this revolutionary?** No. It is convergent with the 2026 consensus architecture — frozen weights plus execution feedback, evaluator co-evolution, counterexample-guided synthesis, hardened grading — and this tree is ahead in refusal discipline but behind frontier systems in measured capability [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663), [2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24206), [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16142). **Is it a sentient creature?** Not measured, not claimed. The in-tree proxy — does experience change competence on a new problem — is negative. The honest operational words are state continuity, self-verification, recovery, and counterfactual understanding.

## 2. First principles: why "once, permanently, anything on a computer" cannot follow

1. **One experience is one constraint, not an identification.** In the real candidate spaces, one published example leaves median 1,037 survivors (10.018 bits). On 479 real sites in this repository, one success licenses zero requirements: the only consistent condition is the empty conjunction, which would act wrongly on 297 / 319 held-out sites (precision 0.069). Two *chosen* observations identify `use.first_computed ∧ use.read_once` at precision 1.000, zero dangerous errors, recall 0.455; all 12 random sequences identified nothing. On 100 held-out HumanEvalFix bugs, 51 had a test-passing candidate and 48 had the correct behaviour somewhere in the candidate set, yet the tests were silent on 43; a chosen question settled 8/8 and majority vote was wrong 3/5. The earlier toy-space measurement (13.05 / 32 survivors, ~2.76 chosen questions) was directionally right but too small to decide software engineering. A chosen question can separate; a repeated one cannot.
2. **A demonstrated action is an operation plus a premise.** Across 600 real applications, stored skills were perfect inside their premise and silent outside it: 5.3% reach for semantic inline, 86.3% for α-conversion, **0 damaged**. The declining mass is not missing experience; it is a missing *proof obligation*. Two trivial operations produced **12** refusal classes from one repository. A finite list of "most advanced actions" with fixed premises cannot cover an unbounded class space.
3. **No single vocabulary covers the space, and a missing observable is not the whole story.** Hand-written families reach 18 / 40; the re-judged cross-corpus learned kinds reach 16 / 40; the verified union is **24 / 40 (60%)**, with **6** only the learned kinds and **8** only the hand-written families. Four claimed learned repairs are **unknown**, not confirmed and not refuted. Vocabulary-free synthesis from the failure reaches 3 / 31 in 0.2 s but adds **0** tasks because the fixes it reaches are already in the hand-written union. The nine no-JSON tasks were made searchable from their test files and **0 / 9** were repaired; three fast-screen passes failed the real pytest. Three vocabularies and the test-file observable have been measured; the ground between them is empty. This is the measured reason not to build a fourth vocabulary by guessing, and not to quote a screen-only result as a repair.
4. **General semantic correctness is undecidable.** Rice's theorem says every non-trivial semantic property of programs is undecidable; formal verification can prove a program correct *relative to a formal specification*, but deciding that the specification captures the user's intention is itself undecidable [4](https://www.marvinzhang.dev/blog/rices-theorem-why-automated-testing-will-fail), [5](https://grokipedia.com/page/Rice's_theorem). Therefore no universal verifier, no finite spec, and no complete list of correctness checks can terminate the open-world problem. The best possible object is a **falsifiable, local, executed witness** — never terminal truth.
5. **Either the space is fixed or the verifier is incomplete.** With a fixed finite list of demonstrated macros, reach is the measure of their premises. If instead the action set is a Turing-complete synthesis language, arbitrary programs become expressible but their correctness relative to intent becomes undecidable. Both branches need the same loop: assemble a new proof obligation from the failure, synthesize an operation/premise for it, and check it with a counterfactual witness.
6. **A pass is a coverage certificate, not correctness.** The tree's real suite rejected **77 / 96** behaviour-altering candidates but let **19** through; a structure-derived law killed **0** of those 19. The interventional goal refused the known `quicksort` false win with **36** subunit-scale violations, yet a memorized observational body that passed **3 / 3** published parenthesization tests could not be refused because only `BOOLEAN` / `TYPE_MATCH` relations were available. A stored stdout verdict accepted **4 / 4** do-nothing programs; a file witness accepted **0 / 4**. Half the clean cross-corpus repairs are not the published fix; they pass the corpus's own tests, which is the only claim made.
7. **Benchmark "success" is harness-dependent.** SWE-Bench Pro V2 repaired or retired **480 / 731** tasks, hardened every verifier, scrubbed git history, and disabled network access after a frontier model copied reference solutions from GitHub on **351** solved tasks, only **237** of which held up with GitHub blocked [2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24206). A leaderboard number is not a proof of the owner's quantifier.

## 3. The single bottleneck, stated so it can be tested

> **Separation**: for the live task, the system must assemble a hypothesis space that contains the right operation **and** an observation that splits the wrong operations from it, at an affordable cost. In an open world, maintaining separation requires synthesizing new operations together with their meaning-preserving premises and new falsifiable verdict relations from executed counterfactuals. That is the proof-carrying counterfactual loop — not a weight, not a list, not a score, and not one organ.

This unifies the earlier answers instead of discarding them:
- **edit space / grammar / learned kinds / synthesis from failure** move `P(truth ∈ H)`;
- **derived operands / instruction policy / observable descriptors** move `P(operation expressible)`;
- **interventional goal / contract / world model** move `P(observation separates)`;
- **admission / file witness / clean-container grading** move `P(oracle is independent and affordable)`;
- **one demonstration** supplies one constraint, and in the newest real-code experiment it supplies **zero licensed requirements**; the condition only appears when a chosen contrast splits the surviving hypotheses.

The newest main-only answer names one factor — **"the observable the decision needs"** — rather than this conjunction. That is the same equation seen from the proof side: 18 sites share all ten static observables and still differ, so the missing observable is the operation's own rewrite-and-compare proof (~55 ms), not another fact lookup. The fastest safe arrangement it measures is **predict (cheap, precision-first) → prove (per-site, exact) → act only if the proof holds**, with the learned condition used as a veto, never as a licence (`WHAT_ONE_DEMONSTRATION_IDENTIFIES_20261116_V1.md`). This is separation with an executable observation, not a rival bottleneck.

PR #6885 supplies the boundary condition for the oracle term: the in-tree fast screen accepted three candidates on the no-JSON tasks that the published pytest refused. A cheap observation can order candidates; only the corpus's own pytest may decide them. Any union number derived from the screen is an upper bound, not a score. This is the same separation equation with the oracle's fidelity made explicit.

The selection result supplies the refusal side of the same term: one surviving behaviour class is not a certificate. On 51 test-passing HumanEvalFix bugs, 43 were unseparated by every available probe; 40 were right and 3 were wrong because the right behaviour had never been generated. The honest outcome is `UNSEPARATED`, not a confident verdict. A version-space selector reuses the tree's existing `finite_identifiability` kernel, asks the cheapest separating question, and refuses when no probe separates. That is separation as maintenance, not a new learner.

The 2026 field converges on the same conjunction. A recursive-self-improvement survey states that "one bottleneck recurs everywhere: the evaluator" and that self-training works where answers are checkable and degrades otherwise [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663). Foresight-governance work finds that world models alone can *amplify* ambiguity; it recommends multi-hypothesis / counterfactual checks rather than confirmation rollouts [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03905v2). Property-guided CEGIS replaces scalar scores with concrete counterexamples [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16142). Continual-ARC found harness-only methods did not gain on unseen tasks while weight-based learning did [7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14505); self-evolving harness agents remain incomplete continual learners [8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25858v1); ASCENT converts one verifier-accepted trajectory into persistent LoRA fast weights only through hindsight self-distillation from a frozen teacher, and direct imitation destabilizes the policy [9](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05303v1). **Every one of those is a separation mechanism, not a "learn once" mechanism.**

It is a corrected first-principles answer, a pytest-rejudged union audit with explicit screen-integrity and refusal boundaries, and an effect-based index of mechanisms already present. It is **not** a composed single-run score, a new learner, a new verifier, a capability registration, a proof of correctness, independent verification, or permission to merge. It does not claim that all computer use is solved; it states exactly why the quantifier is false and what measurement would move it.
